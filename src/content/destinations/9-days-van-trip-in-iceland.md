# 9 days Van trip in Iceland

## **Let’s start**

Infinite panoramic roads, glaciers, waterfalls and volcanos. While going around Iceland it’s just impossible not to be amazed all the time. Every turn reveals a view that looks less like planet Earth and more like a scene from a fantasy movie. 

**Before you go**

* **Visa**: Because Iceland is part of the Schengen Area, if your passport is part of Schengen then you do not need a visa for tourist or short-term business visits.  
* **When to visit**: We decided to visit it in June, with a bigger chance for good weather without being too crowded. Two magical things happen in Iceland during June:  
  * The Lupine Explosion that transforms the countryside in endless fields of purple lupine flowers.  
  * The Midnight Sun: you get roughly 20 hours of daylight. You can use those endless hours of light to hike or explore waterfalls at midnight completely alone. You can make the absolute best out of every single day.

While visiting in winter to see the Northern Lights must be an incredible experience, we truly feel that June is the ultimate month to maximize your time and see the island's raw nature at its finest. Even better than July or August that are more crowded of tourists. 

* **Rent a car / van:** renting a vehicle is a must in order to visit the whole island, since there is no public transport outside big cities.  
  We chose to rent a van in order to be completely free and decide day by day what itinerary to follow. It turned out pretty useful considering the volatility of the weather. Plus, we are van-life lovers\!  
  There are several companies to rent the van from, but for us the best value for money was definitely [Kuku campers](https://www.kukucampers.is/it). It's a local company, our experience was great, and their marketing is absolutely the best: 10/10.   
  The van we chose was VERY basic, without bathrooms or comforts, but there are more fancy options for sure.  
* **Campings:** In the last years it has made **mandatory** to sleep in a camping, you can’t free-camp anymoee; the cost varies between 10-20€ per night per person.   
  You typically don't have to book them in advance, although booking beforehand on at least the same day is recommended.

## **Efficiency tips**

**Transportation from the airport** 

We arrived late at night so we had to take a taxi to a hotel, which is VERY expensive. We didn’t have a choice, since we needed a way to go to a hotel to get some sleep before we could pick up the van the next morning. If you arrive by day and you have to go to Reykjavik we definitely suggest the bus.   
Be aware that all the rental vehicle companies have their offices close to the airport and offer a shuttle option if you arrive during their working hours.

**How to move around the country** 

The only way is definitely to use a car or a van.   
Since Iceland is an expensive country the van will definitely be the cheapest option since you get transportation \+ accommodation with it.   
Of course there exist pre-programmed tours that take you to the main tourist spots, but for our way of travelling this is never an option\! 

**Money** 

Always use your Wise or [Revolut](https://revolut.com/referral/?referral-code=martin_qh_ww83!OCT1-26-AR-L1&geo-redirect) card for the best exchange, they accept cards everywhere.

**SIM card** 

Iceland is part of the European Roaming countries of our providers. With our usual Italian and Swiss SIM cards, we had data normally without anything special required. Check your plan beforehand just in case.

**Clothes** 

Remember that Icelandic weather is quite chaotic even in summer.   
We had days in which we were only wearing a pullover and days with a ski jacket and thermic clothes.   
We suggest bringing only comfortable clothing since the days always require some physical activity.  
The best way is to dress with multiple layers that you can take off if it gets sunnier and warmer.   
Don't forget a windbreaker and rainjacket, since wind will be present most of the days\!

**Parkings** 

Unfortunately again due to overtourism, most tourist attractions in Iceland now have paid parking, a kind of “fee” in order to see natural beauty. They cost between 5 and 10 euros.   
Pay them\! There are cameras everywhere and you will definitely get a fine if you don't, and Iceland is known for their fines not being cheap at all.

## Itinerary 

- Day 1: Golden circle  
- Day 2: Vik Area   
- Day 3: South East   
- Day 4: East fjords   
- Day 5-6: Diamond circle   
- Day 7: North   
- Day 8: Snæfellsnes Peninsula  
- Day 9: Reykjavik 

**Day 1**

The Golden circle is the most touristy part, so it’s not the best part of the island, but it’s the perfect way to start.   
We headed first to the **Þingvellir** National Park. Here you can experience swimming between the tectonic plates, but for sure it’s an expensive tour. We didn't do it.   
Then we headed to the geothermal area of **Geysir**, definitely VERY touristic but it was also cool to witness massive erupting geysers.   
After that, we went to **Gullfoss** waterfall. It’s definitely very beautiful, with a nice walk. We were very lucky with the weather and we also had a rainbow as a cherry on top.   
As you get “temple fatigue” in Asia after some days of only visiting temples, here you will definitely experience “waterfall fatigue”, since there are thousands of them.  
We then passed by the **Kerið** volcanic crater, that definitely is worth a stop.  
We finished this very long day with one of our favorite places of the trip: **Reykjadalur Hot Spring Thermal River**. This place is an absolute must.   
The walk to the river is just stunning (quite long, around 1 hour, so take your time), it seems to be on another planet. When you arrive there is an entire river at 40°, where you can have a bath and warm up while having an *aperitivo*.   
We ended up arriving there at 10 pm but thanks to the never-ending sun we could visit it and enjoy it without too many people.   
This place is a dream and the essence of Iceland itself. 

**Day 2** 

We headed towards the Vík area, where we got ready to walk behind a massive waterfall at **Seljalandsfoss** (our favorite one).  
After that we went to **Skógafoss**. This is for sure one of the most famous, it is worth a stop but it didn't blow our minds. We didn't have the chance to do it due to time constraints, but from there there is a nice walk that takes you to Kvemufoss.    
We then explored the black sand beaches and towering basalt columns of **Reynisfjara**, and had a very nice walk to the viewpoints at **Dyrhólaey**.   
We then drove to Vík and didn't stop by since it’s not so nice. It’s a town built as a stop for tourists.   
For the last activity of the day we drove to **Fjaðrárgljúfur Canyon**. This has been the set for many movies and it’s definitely unique and worth a visit.

**Day 3** 

We took a hike to **Svartifoss**, nice but not mindblowing. The waterfall is different from the others, but if you are tight on time, it’s definitely skippable.   
Afterward, we headed down to **Fjallsárlón**, a lake in front of the glacier, to have one of the greatest experiences ever. We highly recommend doing a kayaking tour in the icebergs of the glacier. We did it with [this](https://www.glacierandvolcano.com/glacier-kayaking-iceland) company, but all of them seem pretty similar in terms of prices and offers.   
The silence is absolute, you can see icebergs rotating and, if you are lucky, even see some seals (we didn't, but some friends did). It’s totally one of those experiences you will remember for the rest of your life.   
After that we went to **Diamond Beach**, one of the most famous on social media. It’s definitely not like the pictures you see online. We suggest stopping for a minute or, if you want to save some money on parking, even skip it.  
We finished the day driving up east with beautiful landscapes and the **Vestrahom** in the background.

**Day 4**  

This was definitely an unlucky day since we had a lot of rain and cold, but this is also part of such a trip.   
We didn't do everything as planned in the East Fjordds because of the weather, so we took it with more calm.   
The drive in the morning was pretty long (4 hours) in order to arrive in the city of **Seyðisfjörður**.   
The drive to arrive there is pretty peculiar and “martian”, and the city itself is very authentic. Because of the rain, we had a nice hot chocolate with some sweets at [Aldan Restaurant](https://maps.app.goo.gl/ub1z29aTXzWppvba8).   
We ended the day at **Stuðlagil Canyon.** But because of the rain and the fog, it wasn’t as shocking as we were expecting. It was very unique though, and it’s worth checking out.   
There were other stops in the plan that we didn't do due to the weather: WWlI US Navy LCM ship wreck walk, Litlanesfoss and Hengifoss. 

**Days 5 and 6** 

Then we headed towards the **Diamond Circle**.   
We started with **Dettifoss**, Europe’s second most powerful waterfall.   
Then we went to the horseshoe-shaped expanse of **Ásbyrgi Canyon**, which you can admire while driving the van and that ends with a small walk.    
Then, we passed by Húsavík where we enjoyed the thermal pools of **Geosea**. Of course very beautiful, but at the same time quite pricey, so we preferred the natural ones. 

The next day the adventure continued around the otherworldly landscapes of **Lake Mývatn** and **Hverfjall** volcano.   
After that, we went to one of the highlights of this trip: **Hverir,** one of Iceland's most active geothermal areas. We wandered through the steaming, bubbling sulfur pits and admired the incredible “martian” landscape. Truly impressive and unique.   
Then we visited **Grjótagjá cave**, very famous thanks to Game of Thrones scenes and walked around the lava labyrinths of **Dimmuborgir**. 

**Day 7**

We started in the morning with (what a news) another impressive waterfall: **Goðafoss**. We know they are a lot, but all of them in this itinerary are unique and worth visiting, we promise.  
From there, we passed through **Akureyri**, where we had a little walk in the cute city center and then continued towards the small fishing village of **Dalvík**, taking in the peaceful landscapes along the way.  
On the way we stopped to visit the charming historic **Grafarkirkja**, Iceland’s oldest turf church.   
To finish the day, we relaxed in the natural hot springs of **Grettislaug**.   
They are definitely off the main road, but we liked them so much because they are very authentic and off-the-beaten-path, surrounded by Iceland’s wild and beautiful scenery. Definitely a must.

**Day 8** 

The last part of our ring road stops at **Snæfellsnes Peninsula.**   
We can definitely say that it was a surprise for us. It’s like an Iceland in miniature, where you can find a variety of Iceland’s geology. This 90-kilometer region compresses moss-covered lava fields, sea cliffs, black sand beaches, and icy volcanoes.  
We definitely suggest the walk from **Arnarstapi** to **Hellnar**. Maybe thanks to the beautiful weather we had that day, we got some of the best pictures and views of the week.   
To see it all it will take one entire day, and then you can search a camp in the area for the night. 

Day 9 

Our last day was in **Reykjavík** for some food and shopping. We had low expectations, but the city definitely gave as a great surprise\! We weren't expenting such a cute city, with cozy restaurants and cafès, and incredible concept and vintage stores.   
Walking through downtown Reykjavík feels like exploring a living art gallery. We suggest just getting lost in the city and enjoying its magic. 

## Food

Iceland is not particularly famous for its gastronomy, yet there are some classic staples you need to try. Since we were on the road all the time, we were always eating on the van or in the middle of nowhere; hence, all the recommendations are in Reykjavík, which was the only time we actually spent time in a city with diverse restaurant options.

* [Bæjarins Beztu Pylsur](https://maps.app.goo.gl/UyMiDR65ACkDL3wv8). The **Icelandic hotdog** is, for some reason, very famous. Also, for some reason, this spot is (by far) the most popular one. It's a good hot dog, but it’s just that… a hotdog. Also, you can likely find the same one for a lower price elsewhere (\~6.50 EUR here). While the sauce and crispy onions in it are really, really good, I guess that's just standard for the Icelandic style. No really sure what’s the reason behind this spot’s massive hype, but it’s worth a try\! (We didn’t have time to try other ones since we were in the city for very short time, but it would be cool to compare different hotdogs for sure\!)  
* [Icelandic Street Food](https://maps.app.goo.gl/JEuj3xE7yz12Jpej9). The price (\~23 EUR) is quite high for a **lamb soup**, but it becomes interesting when you learn that you get unlimited refills. Martín got 5 refills, so \~4.50 EUR per bowl is actually quite good\! And the soup is great: generous amounts of lamb, a tasty broth, and you even get it in a bread bowl. Fun fact: if you have 14 or more refills, you get your Polaroid picture taken and immortalized on the wall. Make sure to arrive hungry if you want to make it into the Hall of Fame\!  
* [Íslenski Barinn \- The Icelandic Bar](https://maps.app.goo.gl/HxjF83pttMQ4MMT59). The ultimate spot if you want to try anything from traditional Icelandic cuisine. You can find every single staple on their menu. Recommendation: the **plokkfiskur** (a popular Icelandic fish stew) for \~29 EUR. Portions were very generous, and food was great. Of course, prices are kind of high but that's normal by Icelandic standards. A nice tip: you get tap water for free without even asking for it, and at the end, they gave us a small chocolate candy that was very tasty too, as a mini dessert, for free. Highly recommended restaurant\!  
* [Brauð & Co](https://maps.app.goo.gl/zw3cpH2JU1o3dzGL6). Hyped shop to have what many consider the best cinnamon rolls in the world. And yes, the pastry lives up to the hype. It’s sure in the top 3 cinnamon rolls we’ve had in our lives ([first one was in Taiwan](https://efficienttravellers.com/destinations/5-days-in-taiwan/)). Just incredible, although \~6 EUR seems a bit excessive for a pastry, but well that's how Icelandic prices go. The spot is small so you should just grab for takeaway while walking around the beautiful city of Reykjavik. Definitely a must try (and as a bonus, the facade of the building is super cool, too).
