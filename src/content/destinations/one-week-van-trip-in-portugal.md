## Let’s start

A van trip, especially along the coast of Portugal, had always been on our bucket list. Travelling by van in Portugal is incredibly easy, since many people do it. Unfortunately, due to a stomach flu and some bad weather, we couldn’t do it exactly the way we had planned, so we limited our trip to the northern part of the coast.

### Before you go

- **When to visit**: Portugal is amazing in every season. We did our trip in December, which turned out to be a great choice in many ways: very few tourists and generally mild temperatures. On the downside, we did get quite a bit of rain.  
  In summer, on the other hand, finding a parking spot for a van can be much more challenging, but the warmer temperatures and better weather will definitely make the experience much better. As for the southern part, we’d love to go in spring, since we couldn’t visit it during this trip.
- **About the van**: There are specific platforms for renting vans, such as [Roadsurfer](https://roadsurfer.com/uk/campervan-hire/portugal/), which tend to be more expensive but offer newer, well-equipped vehicles. If you’re on a budget, you can do what we did and rent through [Yescapa](https://www.yescapa.com/campervan-hire/portugal), where you rent directly from private owners. The vans are usually older, but it’s a more budget-friendly option.  
  If, like us, you don’t plan on staying in campsites, we highly recommend choosing a van with a bathroom inside: it makes a big difference (especially in the colder seasons!).

## Efficiency tips

### Money

Of course, using your Revolut card is always a good idea, but remember that in Portugal, especially if you go off the beaten path, some places (particularly local restaurants) only take cash, so it’s better to always have some with you.

### Transportation from the airport

We landed in Porto, a city with a very convenient metro network. It doesn’t have many stops in the historic city centre, though, so you might end up walking uphill with your luggage. When we went, there was a 70% discount for first-time Bolt users (*VISITPORTO* or *HELLOLISBON*), which got us to the city centre for just 2 EUR.

In general, Bolt is very affordable in both Porto and Lisbon, so it’s definitely an option worth considering.

### Useful apps

As already mentioned, **Bolt** is definitely an app worth using, and it’s generally more affordable here than Uber.

For van travel, **Park4Night** is essential. It’s an amazing app where you can find all the best spots to park overnight (including wild camping), as well as places to refill or empty your water tanks. All campsites are listed too.  
We recommend creating an account, as logging in unlocks all the locations.

## TL;DR

- December means very few tourists and mild temperatures, but expect quite a bit of rain.
- If you won’t sleep in campsites, rent a van with a bathroom inside. Yescapa (private owners) is the budget-friendly option, and Park4Night is a must for overnight spots and water.
- Porto surprised us so much that we ended up preferring it to Lisbon. Don’t miss Pena Palace in Sintra, the waves of Nazaré, and a pastel de nata at Pastéis de Belém.
- Always carry some cash: several of our favourite local restaurants only take cash.

## Authentic experiences

- Watch the sunset from the cliffs near Ericeira

### 1. Porto

Porto truly surprised us. It’s an incredibly beautiful city with that classic Portuguese charm, and full of life. In recent years it has clearly become much more touristy, and honestly, it’s easy to see why. We even ended up preferring it to Lisbon, which says a lot. If you’re planning a trip to Portugal, Porto is absolutely a destination you should include in your itinerary.

The city has a lot to offer. Just get lost walking around the neighbourhoods of Ribeira, Boavista and Bolhão, and enjoy your beautiful surroundings. And don’t miss the iconic picture of the beautiful, old-school trams that go around the city!

A must-do: take an evening walk along the Ribeira waterfront at sunset, with the view of the Luís I Bridge. Then enjoy a glass of incredible port wine in one of the many wine cellars around the area (check out the [Food](#food) section for our recommendation!).

And lastly, a fun fact: what is considered the world’s most beautiful McDonald’s is in Porto. You can find it [here](https://maps.app.goo.gl/b7ZG4kgxtxcmdBGv8). The architecture is indeed stunning (and quite bizarre for a McDonald’s), but don’t expect anything out of this world. It’s just worth seeing if you’re passing by the area.

### 2. Coimbra and Nazaré

Coimbra offers a completely different atmosphere compared to Porto and Lisbon, with a more relaxed and authentic feel. It is known as Portugal’s historic university city, and it’s perfect for wandering through its narrow streets, especially in the area around the university. Coimbra just has a very unique charm.

Nazaré is a small seaside town that has gained worldwide fame for its giant waves. There’s a very nice viewpoint at Miradouro do Suberco, but the absolute must is the Forte de São Miguel Arcanjo, where you can witness the incredible power of the Atlantic Ocean. Even outside the peak surf season the views are breathtaking, and if you’re lucky enough to catch the massive waves, it’s a view you’ll never forget.

### 3. Lisbon

We got two full days of rain in Lisbon, so we couldn’t really do much. Still, we think two days is a good amount of time for the city.

We really suggest wandering through the historic districts of Alfama and Bairro Alto, since they’re the cutest parts of the city. Also, only a 20-minute drive from the centre, there’s Belém, with its iconic tower on the Tagus River. And don’t miss the breathtaking views from the city’s many *miradouros*.

In Belém you need to stop and try the *pastel de Belém*, by far the best pastel de nata of our lives. Literally perfect. (Check out the [Food](#food) section!)

A famous activity is riding the popular Tram 28 to see the city. However, the queue was endless, so we skipped it: it seemed like a very touristy attraction, and not really worth it with so much rain and fog.

We weren’t very lucky on this trip. The initial plan was to drive through the Algarve all the way to the very south, but we caught a very bad stomach flu, so we had to change plans and stay in the north to move less and have a more chill road trip.

### 4. Sintra and Cascais

A visit to Sintra is definitely a MUST on your road trip: it feels like stepping into a fairytale. Just outside Lisbon, it’s an enchanted town filled with mystical places and lush gardens. Visiting the colourful Pena Palace is mandatory: it feels unreal that such a beautiful palace exists. If you manage to see it on a sunny day, that’s definitely a plus.

Afterwards, we headed to Cascais, where the vibe shifted completely. We really, really liked the area, especially walking along the seaside promenade at sunset. Be aware that the town is kind of "fancy", so it’s pricier than other places in Portugal.

### 5. Ericeira and Óbidos

Ericeira is one of those places that instantly makes you slow down and enjoy life. Known as a world-class surf destination, it combines crazy cliffs, powerful waves and a very chill vibe. Even if you’re not into surfing, the ocean views alone are worth the visit, especially at sunset. It’s a really small town, so we don’t suggest staying more than one day if you’re not going surfing.

Óbidos is a very different spot: a village that feels frozen in time. It is kind of fake and built for tourism, but it’s very nice anyway and full of cute shops. We didn’t go into the castle, but they say it offers incredible views of the surrounding countryside.

### 6. Peniche and Aveiro

Peniche is another surf destination. We didn’t really like the town itself, so we suggest stopping by only if you’re really interested in trying some new surf spots.

Continuing north, Aveiro offers a completely different atmosphere. Often called the "Venice of Portugal", the city is defined by its canals and colourful boats. Definitely a cute city to stop by for a few hours.

### 7. Arouca and the N222 scenic route

For those seeking nature and nice landscapes, Arouca is a cute stop. The region is home to the 516 Arouca bridge, one of the longest pedestrian suspension bridges in the world.

We spent the night in Arouca so we could start driving along the famous N222 in the morning. We’d read that it’s considered one of the most beautiful roads in the world, running through the Douro Valley past vineyards, hills and amazing viewpoints. It’s definitely beautiful, but it’s not *that* unique: calling it one of "the most beautiful in the world" is a bit much, in our opinion.

Still, it’s a must on a van trip through this beautiful country, so we definitely suggest doing it.

## Food

- [Morro d’amores](https://maps.app.goo.gl/qheHegMduZJ5JGDPA) (Porto). A charming, intimate spot for a port wine tasting. The owner truly made our night: one of the friendliest Portuguese people we met. Prices were very fair: around 6 EUR per glass of wine and 15 EUR for a board with bread, crackers, olive oil and cheese. **(Beware: they only take cash here!)**
- [Taxca](https://maps.app.goo.gl/7NbXDYuzuQfAeav3A) (Porto). A very local, easy-going spot for a quick (and possibly very unhealthy!) snack, drinks, or even a full meal if you order a few dishes. Prices are absurdly low: you can get a bifana for 3.50 EUR and a MASSIVE portion of fries with fried eggs and ham (excellent-quality ham) for 12 EUR. The vibe is very relaxed, with no table service or anything fancy. Make sure to arrive early or be ready to wait: there was a very long queue when we went. **(Beware: they only take cash here!)**
- [Negra Café Boavista](https://maps.app.goo.gl/A2Q58MJfCawXcYNQ6) (Porto). One of the best breakfasts/brunches of our lives. No exaggeration. This place is INSANE! The portions were huge, but everything was so good that we could have eaten thousands of dishes.
- [Mercado Bom Sucesso](https://maps.app.goo.gl/cEJUF3ECpGqGX7N47) (Porto). A food hall with a varied mix of food stalls, restaurants and bars. Very convenient if you’re travelling in a group and people want to try different kinds of food. It can be hard to find a table, since there aren’t many and it’s usually pretty busy, but the food is quite good.
- [Café Santiago](https://maps.app.goo.gl/RmSGYXQ1YUspdi8X9) (Porto). You need to try a **francesinha** on this trip, and this place is supposed to serve one of the best in the city. Extremely long queue (we ordered it to take away because it was impossible to sit down), and quite pricey. The francesinha was good, but it felt a bit like a tourist trap. It’s a classic anyway, so it’s probably worth a try, but we’re pretty sure there are other places with lower prices that offer the same or even better quality, although we didn’t have time to try another one.
- [Restaurante Maria Portuguesa](https://maps.app.goo.gl/D6TRhE8LnnYPi6NW8) (Coimbra). As authentic and homemade as it gets. This spot really feels like eating in a Portuguese mom’s kitchen. The **açorda de bacalhau** (bread and codfish stew) for just 10.70 EUR was by far the highlight.
- [Taberna Carolina](https://maps.app.goo.gl/azmmnWGBCob1ygzDA) (Lisbon). We had the bad luck of arriving on the exact day they changed the menu, and 85% of it wasn’t available, so we couldn’t really test this place. It seems to be a local staple, so we’d suggest visiting anyway: it’s probably very good on a normal day.
- [Taberna Sal Grosso São Bento](https://maps.app.goo.gl/9XsYnipGXquWDAN18) (Lisbon). Away from the most central, touristy area, this restaurant has everything it takes to be perfect. This place is a must-try in Lisbon, hands down!
- [Pastéis de Belém](https://maps.app.goo.gl/tazwLX3gbTdLZvcE9) (Belém). The original and legendary **pastéis de nata**. Not much to say: they’re just perfect. Definitely a must.
- [Pastelaria Gregório](https://maps.app.goo.gl/dTnZ2CXzHXUHnvRB6) (Sintra). One of the few non-tourist traps in the area. Very affordable prices, friendly staff and great quality. Come here to try the popular Sintra treats: **travesseiros** and **queijadas**.
- [Taberna da Esquina](https://maps.app.goo.gl/QHhGPgTpBWmNXiqN8) (Cascais). An incredible local spot. As homemade and authentic as it gets: the menu was just a few pencil-written lines in a small notebook. The **lulinhas à Algarvia** (Algarve-style baby squid) were so tasty and so abundant, and for just 13 EUR! Tourists probably don’t know about this place since it’s quite far from any tourist spot, so it’s hands-down a hidden gem. **(Beware: they only take cash here!)**
- [Tasca do Amarelinho](https://maps.app.goo.gl/wiH8nb7CrgqdwA2p7) (Aveiro). Come here for a great **feijoada de leitão** (suckling pig and bean stew). The portion was massive, and it even came with white rice on the side. The taste was flawless, and it was only 13.50 EUR. Other things on the menu looked amazing too (the bifanas must be great, and they’re super cheap as well), but we only stayed for one lunch in Aveiro (otherwise, we would definitely have come back!).
- [Pastelaria Ramos](https://maps.app.goo.gl/5wEHmvXAaQzLqw6u7) (Aveiro). Packed with locals, and it’s easy to see why. Everything was SO good, and prices are ridiculously low. The croissant was (surprisingly) life-changing. The **ovos moles** were great too, although the concept may not be for everyone (but it’s definitely a classic). We were there around Christmas, so we had the chance to try their **bolo-rei** (king cake), and it was SO good too. This place is just incredible: you must stop by and buy as many things as you can.

There are some other spots that were on our list but, due to time constraints and the stomach flu, we didn’t manage to visit. We’ll leave them here anyway so you can give them a try!
